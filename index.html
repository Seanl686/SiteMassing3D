🏠 Stepped Half / Sectional Footprint

Ridge offset nudges the solved ridge off where the two pitches put it. Ridge step lifts the rear peak clear of the front one, opening a clerestory wall between them — negative lifts the front instead. Once the peaks differ there is no ridge to meet at, so the taller plane sails past it and hangs over the low roof.

A split-pitch roof runs a different pitch on the rear slope, so the ridge sits off the centreline and one side of the peak is higher and shorter than the other. Leave the rear pitch blank for a symmetrical gable.

Ridge position decides what gives when the two pitches disagree. Where the pitches meet slides the ridge off centre and keeps both wall heights. Locked to home centre keeps the peak dead centre for the whole length and raises the shallower side's wall instead, so the two planes still meet at one peak.

Sections Along The Length

Split the home into sections measured from the left end. Each carries its own pitches, ridge, eave heights and style, so one part of the house sits at a different pitch and peak than the next. Set-in pulls that part's front or rear wall inward — set both equally and that half is narrower all round; a negative value pushes it out so that part runs deeper than the rest. The roof, the gable end, the skirting and any porch on that stretch all follow the moved wall, the long walls turn a return at the step, and where two roofs disagree the gap between them is walled in.

BMP Porches & Wall Bump-Outs

▼

Where the plan stops being a rectangle. Two dimension lines that disagree — 58'-8" one side, 56'-0" the other — is a recess; a +16" note is a bump-out; a 6' PORCH drawn inside the outline is a covered deck tucked under the main roof.

Depth is signed: positive projects out past the wall, negative cuts a recess in. Offset runs left→right as seen from outside, same as an opening.

Match The Real Home

A typed hex is a guess, and the guess always comes out too clean — real siding photographs greyer and cooler than its swatch name, and a roof read by eye lands a couple of shades light. Pick the colours straight off the photograph instead and the render is one for one with the unit.

HOM Photos Of The Real Home

▼

Not the lot — the home itself. Dealer-lot or catalogue shots of this model, one per wall. These are what the finished render must look like: the real siding profile and colour, the real window proportions, the trim, the roof as photographed. The massing plates stay the authority on where everything is; each photo is laid over its matching plate, not used instead of it.

Load the ones you have. Walls you cannot photograph are exactly why the plates exist — the package tells the model to follow the plate and the written spec there, and to invent nothing.
